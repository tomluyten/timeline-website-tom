About

I am an artist, researcher, designer and musician. I use technology as a material to shape the way we interact with the world and each other.

I lead the research group Resilient Design Practice at Zuyd University of Applied Sciences in Maastricht. As creative director of RipperDocs I design masks for artists. My wife Tahnee and I make conceptual work under the alias Machine Mother, and I play in the hardcore punk band Pink Ballet Blackout.

This website is an index of my work as it evolves over time.

  1. 2026
    The ever changing shape of the design practice to comeNADR, with Karin Slegers, Theo Ploeg and Raoul Boers.research001

    As design spreads beyond the professional discipline, through design thinking and now generative AI, the act of making is no longer the designer's exclusive domain, while societal transitions pull designers into new roles.

    This paper describes four roles for the professional designer: systems thinker, connector and convener, designer and maker, leader and storyteller, derived from a report from the Design Council. Their foundation is Bildung: mastery combined with responsibility.

    Contribution to the NADR knowledge cycle 2026, "If everybody designs, what makes a designer?", presented at Dutch Design Week.

  2. 2025
    Founded research center Resilient Design PracticeResearch group on design at Zuyd University of Applied Sciences.research002

    I founded and lead Resilient Design Practice, the research group (lectoraat) on design at Zuyd University of Applied Sciences in Maastricht. It generates knowledge for a resilient design practice: a practice that changes shape with every cycle, without losing its core.

    Generative AI pushes the designer away from making as an exclusive domain, while societal transitions pull them towards new roles and responsibilities. The group studies new methods by putting them into practice: systems thinking, narrative and speculative design, and thinking with your hands. Our position: democratization does not make the professional designer redundant, but more necessary than ever.

    Our Machine Mother is the shared practice of Tom Luyten and Tahnee Mertens. It challenges and questions the relationship between people and technology. How they repel and attract each other in this love/hate triangle.

  4. This Circle is ViciousA tower of screens caught in an endless loop.art004

    This Circle is Vicious turns the gallery upside down with a totem of discarded computer screens showing three endlessly reconfiguring video streams: Destruction in pulsing black-red, Creation in high-contrast black-white, and a live webcam feed glitched into a digital mirror. All imagery is crafted by Tom Luyten using his own footage, generative software, and AI models, weaving an ever-shifting narrative in which humans and technology are both builders and destroyers of each other.

    A multi-layered soundtrack by composer Jaimy Kortenhoff drives the visual storm. Each layer reshapes itself in real time, so the sonic texture is never the same—a living composition that grows, implodes, and rises again. Image and sound interlock like cogs: one moment whispering drones suggest construction, the next a burst of noise tears everything down, while the screen colors echo or clash. The result is an endless cycle of love and annihilation between man and machine.

    Keep circling the work and you quite literally experience the vicious loop: with every step the story shifts and you become part of the drama anew.

    Made for the exhibition Our Machine Mother, as the physical counterpart of the AR work from 2020.

    Type
    Installation, live visuals, sound
    Sound
    Jaimy Kortenhoff
    Shown at
    Expo: Our Machine Mother, Artichoque Art Gallery, Maastricht, May 2025

  5. Value is in the Eye of the BeholderAn artwork whose price depends on your attention.art005

    Value is in the Eye of the Beholder consists of three gleaming acrylic displays that, together with a digital screen, show their price in real time. Every second you keep looking, the value shoots up; avert your gaze and the amount drops instantly. You shift from spectator to co-creator: your attention is the currency.

    This playful yet incisive installation exposes the attention economy in which likes, views, and hype determine what art is “worth.” By making the price visibly rise and fall, the work confronts us with the volatility of status and market—and with our own role in that constantly oscillating valuation.

    In Maastricht—a city of museums, festivals, and centuries-old heritage—it poses a provocative question: who or what sets cultural value here? Value is in the Eye of the Beholder invites residents, visitors, and professionals to focus their gaze more consciously and to explore whether a more sustainable appreciation of art is possible, beyond the whims of the moment.

    Type
    Interactive installation
    Shown at
    Expo: Our Machine Mother, Artichoque Art Gallery, Maastricht, May 2025

  6. Synthetic RealitiesA Mars landing that never happened, on a sixties television.art006

    Synthetic Realities airs a Mars landing that never happened on a 1960s television set. Black-and-white footage, analogue static and delayed voices conjure the nostalgia of an era when TV was synonymous with truth—even as the viewer instantly realises the broadcast is pure fiction.

    The work exposes our fragility in the post-truth age: if even “authentic” video can be effortlessly generated by AI, what is left of visual evidence? Visitors are challenged to distrust their own perception and to ask how reality and illusion can still be separated when algorithms shape the narrative.

    For Maastricht—a city of heritage, stories and international influences—this piece serves as a provocative mirror. Synthetic Realities questions how a place that prides itself on historical authenticity can hold its ground amid deepfakes and media noise, inviting residents to remain critical, resilient and media-savvy in the digital realm.

    Type
    Installation, video
    Shown at
    Expo: Our Machine Mother, Artichoque Art Gallery, Maastricht, May 2025

  7. Ora et LaboraKneel, type your profession, and see how AI could take over your work.art007

    Ora et Labora literally puts you on your knees: settle onto an old prie-dieu, type your profession into the screen, and watch an AI unravel which tasks your role could soon surrender to machines. The irony of a hymn to labour that simultaneously predicts your own redundancy renders the experience both playful and unsettling.

    The installation magnifies our rising faith in technology and its impact on work and self-worth. As the system dissects your career, it forces you to ask: what remains of our identity when algorithms take the helm?

    Type
    Interactive installation
    Shown at

  8. Machine dialogues: CrownsA glitched crown of thorns, in two versions.art008

    A single AI-generated base image served as the starting point for this work. After this digital raw diamond was cleaned up and rearranged into a new composition, hand-drawn lines and textures filled the gaps, forging a hybrid picture where mechanical perfection meets human spontaneity.

    The result was then fed through custom glitch software that injected further disruptions and distortions. Order collides once more with chaos, giving the image a final, unpredictable layer. Crowns lays bare the ongoing dialogue between control and chance, algorithm and hand, inviting viewers to consider where authorship ends and new meaning begins to take shape.

    Shown at the Our Machine Mother exhibition and sold through Artichoque Art Gallery, Maastricht.

  9. Machine dialogues: CrashGlitches in black, white and red, in two versions.art009

    Two alu-dibond prints reveal a fragmented landscape of swirling colors and shapes. Within the collage, AI-generated imagery merges with hand-drawn lines, nuances, and textures—a collision of machine precision and human touch. Cracks, pixel trails, and distorted edges betray an aesthetic implosion that is as gripping as it is unsettling.

    This final image emerged only after the “finished” piece was passed through custom glitch software and deliberately deformed. Crash thus exposes the tension between creation and destruction, control and surrender. It invites viewers to consider where authorship ends: when is an artwork complete, and when does it begin to disintegrate—or might that be the very moment when new meaning takes shape?

    Shown at the Our Machine Mother exhibition and sold through Artichoque Art Gallery, Maastricht.

  10. Machine dialogues: Direct meA silhouette against red light, in two versions.art010

    A diptych presents the silhouette of a woman, delicately edged by a red-glowing light against an otherwise dark backdrop. The sparse illumination reveals only contours; body and shadow merge into a quiet image that echoes the moody aesthetic of Machine Mother.

    The unseen director is an AI chatbot, specially trained to guide the model step by step in pose, framing, and lighting. The photographer served merely as facilitator while the algorithm made the artistic decisions. The work questions the boundaries of authorship and control—where does human creativity end and the machine’s begin?

  38. Bravest momentBike rides turned into art.art038

    Bravest moment, also called Bike art, takes the data of a bike ride and turns it into an artwork. Each print sits halfway between a graph, where you can spot the highs of the ride, and an abstract composition to hang on the wall.

    It started with route prints, such as a ride through the Pyrenees and the 3 Ballons, with top speed, highest point and time worked into the design. With Watersley sports and talent park it grew into a system: every classification winner of the Watersley Ladies Challenge (2022) received her own print, drawn from the route and race data.
